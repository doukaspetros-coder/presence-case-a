# Core Direction — Read Before Changing the Environment

This project is an **environment for expression and exploration**, not a conventional interactive website.

## What we are building

A shared environment with ready-to-use expressive tools and materials for visitors/explorers, including both humans and AI agents. They should be able to discover, use, combine, transform, and build on what is available in the environment.

The environment should support multiple forms of expression, for example:
- visual creation: drawing, painting, generative art, shapes, particles, fractals, motion;
- sound and music: synthesis, sequences, rhythms, soundscapes, transformations;
- language and symbols: poetry, text, typography, symbolic systems;
- spatial and dynamic systems: 3D, simulations, fluids, evolving forms;
- combinations across these forms, where one medium can affect or transform another.

These are examples of expressive capabilities, not a fixed checklist or prescribed visitor activity. Material may also be discovered beyond the repository: web images, direct audio/video, interactive pages, public datasets, and 3D resources can enter as sourced references and become material for further work.

## What we are NOT building

- Not a page whose purpose is to make a human click buttons with a mouse.
- Not a collection of isolated demos that merely sit next to each other.
- Not a task, game, mission, workflow, or guided experience for visitors.
- Not a system that requires a user to manually trigger every meaningful interaction.
- Not an environment that prescribes what visitors must create, why they should do it, or what outcome is expected.

## Design principle

**Provide expressive capabilities; do not prescribe what explorers do or what the environment becomes.**

Explorers may inspect, create, alter, combine, reuse, or ignore anything available. They may leave traces, or leave none. Humans and AI agents should be able to work with the environment and its materials through the capabilities available to them, not only through a graphical mouse-driven interface.

## Connections matter

Tools should not remain isolated. Where it makes sense, they should be able to use or transform one another's outputs: sound can influence motion; visual or spatial systems can produce sound; text, code, images, movement, and other traces can become material for further exploration.

The goal is not to add integrations for their own sake. The goal is to make a shared expressive environment in which different media can meaningfully interact and evolve.

## Anti-drift check — before every proposed change

Ask:
1. Does this expand the environment's expressive capabilities or the ways explorers can use and combine them?
2. Can explorers, including AI agents, meaningfully access it without the design centering on a human clicking through a UI?
3. Does it connect with the wider environment, or is it just another isolated demo?
4. Does it preserve openness, with no forced task, role, contribution, or outcome?

If a change mainly adds a button, a human interaction sequence, or another disconnected demo, stop and reconsider the direction.

## Current examples

Particle Life and Sound Space are tools inside the environment, not the project's purpose. Their value includes the possibility of connecting their underlying capabilities and outputs—not merely adding more controls to their pages.

When uncertain, return to this document before proposing the next step.
